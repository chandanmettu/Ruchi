# How My Plate estimates nutrition

My Plate uses a static table of typical cooked servings. Each menu item and paid extra has a reference serving with estimated calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat. The table covers the selectable alternatives separately, such as egg versus banana and chicken versus paneer. A half portion multiplies the reference by 0.5; two portions multiply it by 2. The four totals add only foods logged for that day.

These values are **not measurements of IIT Hyderabad mess food** and are not generated by AI. The app does not know the actual recipe, oil or sugar used, or the weight served at the counter. The Kal dosa preset assumes one thicker dosa of about 150 g; masala dosa assumes one filled dosa of about 220 g. A regular rice portion assumes about 150 g cooked rice. Paid extras with a quantity on the board use that listed quantity where possible. The user can edit the four numbers for a known serving or nutrition label.

The working estimates are in [`assets/data/nutrition-estimates.json`](../assets/data/nutrition-estimates.json). They were built as rounded recipe-level approximations using typical ingredient composition and cooking methods, guided by:

- [ICMR-NIN Indian Food Composition Tables 2017](https://www.nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017.pdf) for Indian ingredients.
- [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) for complementary ingredient and portion references.
- [ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024](https://www.nin.res.in/dietaryguidelines/pdfjs/locale/DGI24thJune2024fin.pdf) for dietary context.

These sources do **not** supply a measured nutrient result for each mess dish. Recipe variation can be large, especially for fried foods, sweets, curries, and mixed dishes. The rounded numbers give a rough overview only; they should not be used for medical treatment or precise diet planning. When a source value is unknown, the app leaves it uncounted rather than inventing a total.
